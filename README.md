# Fix-it

**A community and service network connecting practical help, trusted providers and accessible payment.**

> Use what you have to get the help you need — and move forward.

**Status:** concept and specification · **Founder:** Miha Tavčar  
**Scope:** physical services, digital work and community assistance

[Vision](docs/VISION.md) · [Roadmap](docs/ROADMAP.md) · [Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md) · [Korako Yolawani](https://github.com/vcheeko/korako-yolawani)

## Why Fix-it?

A driver needs a repair abroad but has no locally accepted payment method. A family needs a safe roof but cannot fund the work. A person has a business idea but lacks the tools and expertise to carry it forward.

Fix-it aims to connect each need with qualified help, a clear agreement, a supported way to pay or fund the work, and evidence of the outcome.

## The proposed experience

1. **Describe the need.** Explain the situation and desired result.
2. **Find a suitable route.** Match the job with qualified providers or a community assistance process.
3. **Agree the work and cost.** See scope, acceptance criteria, budget and payment conditions.
4. **Follow execution.** Track progress and approve material changes.
5. **Verify and settle.** Check the result, complete the agreed payment and retain a path for complaints.

These are design goals, not a service available through this repository today.

## One account, connected capabilities

| Component | Intended role |
| --- | --- |
| **Fix-it network** | Qualified providers, service standards, agreements and dispute handling. |
| **Fix-it currency** | A proposed cryptocurrency for service payments across participating projects. |
| **Fix-it Community** | Verified assistance funded with money, materials, equipment or donated professional time. |
| **Korako Yolawani / Mira** | Understand the goal and coordinate agents, tools and services through a continuous human journey. |

The intended Korako experience is one account and one view of work and spending. Users should not need separate subscriptions with every model or tool provider. Existing user-owned tools can be optional connections. This integration is proposed, not implemented.

## A network with standards

Provider membership would require identity checks, relevant qualifications, clear job agreements and continuing quality review. Ratings should come from confirmed jobs. Complaints must have a defined inspection and remediation process.

For physical work, relevant professional inspection may be necessary. AI-generated approval cannot establish that a vehicle, roof or electrical installation is safe.

## Payment that serves the job

The ambition is to accept supported forms of value while providers receive a clearly quoted payout. Conversion and settlement would be arranged behind the user experience through suitable infrastructure and partners.

Fix-it cryptocurrency is part of the long-term vision. **No token has been issued by this repository; chain, ticker, supply and contract are undecided.** A quoted service payout and the market value of a traded token are different. No token appreciation, universal acceptance or loss-free conversion is promised.

## Community help

People could contribute funds, materials, transport, equipment or professional time. Receiving help or volunteering should not require buying cryptocurrency.

Projects would use verified needs, approved budgets, qualified work and transparent reporting that protects recipients' privacy. Help should not depend on publicity or endorsement.

## Start with evidence

The first proposed milestone is a digital-task simulation:

**Quote → approve → reserve test balance → execute → verify → settle once → release unused funds**

It should prove recovery, cancellation and prevention of duplicate charges before real money is introduced. See the [roadmap and acceptance criteria](docs/ROADMAP.md).

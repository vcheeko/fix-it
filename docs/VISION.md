# Fix-it vision and boundaries

## Purpose

Help people turn available resources into practical help. Fix-it combines a proposed service network, a service-use cryptocurrency and a community for assistance across physical and digital projects.

The long-term ambition is international, always-accessible coordination. Real coverage must be stated by location, service category, payment method and operating hours.

## Initial focus: roadside assistance through existing providers

Fix-it's first intended service scope is automotive assistance: roadside help, towing, repair and onward-travel coordination through existing qualified providers. It does not plan an owned towing fleet or repair shops. Insurance is a later partner capability, not a prerequisite for this first scope.

Korako and Mira would coordinate the person's goal and approvals; Fix-it would connect assistance and payment. Supported source assets should remain separate until the user approves a conversion. The provider must see the net payout, currency, timing and conditions.

Payment capability is designed first. No separate Korako coin is planned: a future Fix-it coin could serve participating projects if it demonstrates useful benefits. Users and providers must retain suitable supported payment and payout choices.

## Illustrative journeys

| Situation | Proposed response | Evidence of completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driver needs a repair abroad | Find a qualified provider; agree repair and supported payment; arrange assistance | Job record, relevant inspection, receipt and complaint route |
| Household needs roof repairs | Verify scope; obtain a qualified quote; arrange payment or community funding | Work record and qualified inspection where required |
| User needs a digital project | Korako coordinates agents and tools within an approved budget | Deliverable, independent verification and measured usage |

These scenarios are examples, not active offerings.

## Service agreement

Each job should specify scope, materials, price, deadlines, acceptance criteria, cancellation terms and responsibility for defects. Change orders require explicit agreement. Provider reviews must be tied to confirmed transactions, with fraud reporting and appeal.

Workmanship guarantees, complaints handling and insurance are separate mechanisms. Insurance ambitions depend on appropriate partners and defined coverage, exclusions, territories, limits and claims procedures. No coverage is offered here.

## Currency and settlement

Fix-it is envisioned as a cryptocurrency usable for services across participating projects. Users should not need to arrange their own exchange for each provider.

The system still requires supported assets, price sources, quote expiry, fees, liquidity, custody decisions and settlement arrangements. A market price alone does not establish support or liquidity.

Quoted provider payouts should identify currency, net amount and settlement conditions. Fixed payouts require a funded mechanism and an accountable party. A traded token has market risk; preserving nominal value, purchasing power and value against all currencies are different claims and are not guaranteed.

Architecture should keep the job ledger independent of payment methods, so test credit, payment partners and any future token can use separate adapters.

No blockchain, token economics, contract address, allocation, fundraising terms or exchange listing is selected in this specification. Live activities require assessment of the specific jurisdictions and operating model before launch.

## Community

Assistance can be funded through voluntary donations, sponsorship and a transparently defined share of network revenue. Donated skills and materials matter as much as money.

Proposed process: consented request, local verification, agreed priorities, qualified scope, funded plan, delivery, inspection and privacy-preserving reporting.

Aid funds should have separate accounting. Recipients must not be required to purchase tokens, appear in publicity or endorse the network. The public repository is not a channel for personal assistance applications or donations today.

## Relationship with Korako

Korako should retain the user's goal, project state, tool routing, approvals and verification. Fix-it would handle service and payment coordination through a defined interface.

A job identifier should link the quote, approval, usage, evidence and settlement. Accounting must distinguish measured usage, calculated cost, user estimates and unavailable data.

The user sees one account and a coherent work history. Provider credentials, personal records and private operational data do not belong in public logs.

## Publication boundaries

This repository contains public documentation and may later contain reviewed synthetic examples. Private implementation, customer data, keys, contracts and security-sensitive records are excluded.

Publishing a concept does not establish a software license for future code. Licensing for reusable implementation should be chosen explicitly.

## Open design questions

- Which first service category and location have reliable pilot partners?
- Which currencies and payment methods can actually settle at acceptable cost?
- Who verifies contested work, and who funds remedies?
- What concrete problem requires a transferable token rather than ordinary accounting?
- How can community assistance remain sustainable and fairly allocated?
